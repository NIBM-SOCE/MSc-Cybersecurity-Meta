# Speaker script, 15 minutes

Timings are cumulative. Slide 13 (sources) is not presented. The appendix is used only for questions.

## 1. Title (0:00 to 0:45)
Good morning. In the next fifteen minutes I will make one proposal and answer the three questions your
partnerships framework asks of it. The proposal is to franchise the University of Essex MSc Cyber
Security, with the Postgraduate Diploma as an exit award, for evening and weekend delivery at NIBM in
Colombo. The three questions are whether NIBM is a suitable partner, whether the demand is real, and
whether the programme can be delivered and scaled. I will take them in that order.

## 2. A statutory institution since 1976 (0:45 to 2:00)
NIBM was founded in 1968 with UNDP and ILO support and incorporated by Act of Parliament in 1976. It
is a state-owned institute under a ministry, with campuses in Colombo, Kurunegala, Kandy, Galle and
Matara. I mention the dates not because age is the argument, but because permanence and accountability
are: a statutory body cannot quietly disappear, rebrand or be sold, which I understand is the failure
mode UK universities most fear in transnational education. Computing teaching began here in 1980, and
foreign university awards in 1996.

## 3. Thirty years of delivering external awards (2:00 to 3:15)
Every degree NIBM delivers today already runs under an external awarding body's quality oversight.
Coventry University degrees since 2017, University College Dublin before that, and vendor-standard
curricula through Cisco, Red Hat, AWS and EC-Council academies, and BCS accreditation on site. External examiners, moderation and partner audit are routine here. A franchise
is a familiar operating model for us, not a new one. NIBM matches each award to the partner strongest in
it, which is why several names appear on this slide.

## 4. Why Essex: top of the Colombo field (3:15 to 4:30)
Five UK universities' cyber master's degrees are delivered through Sri Lankan partners today. On the
2026 Complete University Guide and Guardian tables, Essex is highest by a wide margin. Essex is sixth in
the UK for research power in computer science. Its own award portfolio already carries the 120-credit
PgDip exit we are asking for. And the campus programme's profile, security by design, digital identity,
digital forensics and network security, is the natural continuation of our Ethical Hacking and Network
Security degree. We have stated NCSC status honestly: provisional, ahead of three local rivals not on the
list at all, behind Westminster. What may be claimed for a franchise is yours to decide, and we will
follow that exactly.

## 5. One computing base, and no rung above Level 6 (4:30 to 5:45)
This is our base. About 120 computing graduates a year across three programmes, 346 in the last six
convocations, and none of them has a postgraduate route at NIBM. The cyber stream itself is small at
graduation today, around twenty a year, because the early intakes were small. Degree intake at SOCE is
entry to year three, and those entries have jumped from 17 to 51 per intake, so the direct pool roughly
doubles from 2027. A curriculum review and rename to Applied Cybersecurity is pending. So the programme
is pitched as the postgraduate exit for the whole computing base, with two entry lanes I will come to.

## 6. Demand rises with a UK award (5:45 to 7:00)
We asked 57 students and graduates. 74% were likely or very likely to enrol in a NIBM postgraduate cyber
programme. Told the award would come from a UK university, that rose to 84%. Twenty-three people moved
up and nobody moved down; the "very likely" box nearly doubled. Why? This is a market raised on GCE
Ordinary and Advanced Levels, with Edexcel and Cambridge running through the British Council and the
international schools. A UK award is the familiar currency. The limits are on the slide: a self-selected
internal sample, two days, no fee tested. Treat the direction as solid and the magnitude as slightly
generous.

## 7. The affordability corridor (7:00 to 8:15)
This slide does two jobs. Essex's own two products cost a Sri Lankan student about eleven million rupees
for campus tuition alone and about 5.9 million online, against a local field priced between 350 and 780
thousand. The rupee has gone from 264 to about 450 to the pound since 2022. Entry-level IT pay starts
near 80,000 rupees a month, so the online fee is six years of it. That is the honest answer to how a
franchise sits beside Essex Online: it serves people who cannot buy either. The second job: the local
labour market now produces about 17,000 IT graduates a year, entry-level supply is no longer the
bottleneck, and AI is compressing generalist roles. The rational response for a graduate is to specialise,
and a UK master's priced locally is the only route within reach.

## 8. Committed demand, discounted in the open (8:15 to 9:30)
Forty-one of the 57 are positive on a UK award and could start within a year. We do not present that as
demand. Apply standard purchase-intent weights and it becomes 29 central, 21 conservative: rates of 50%
and 37.5%. Apply those by lane. The direct lane, today's twenty cyber graduates, gives seven to ten. The
bridged lane, the other hundred computing graduates, with the rate halved again for bridge friction,
gives nineteen to twenty-five. Together, 26 to 35 per intake, before recent alumni, working
professionals, or the doubling of the direct lane. For scale, the state university's own cyber master's
took 28 in its 2023 intake.

## 9. Industry describes a postgraduate gap (9:30 to 10:30)
Seven senior people outside NIBM, from IT services, telecom, insurance and its regulator, and three
national universities. All seven expect demand to rise; six of seven rate a UK master's an advantage in
hiring; six of seven volunteered to contribute to delivery; none refused to consider sponsoring staff. Their words
matter more than the counts. Graduates can identify vulnerabilities but struggle to validate findings,
weigh business risk and recommend fixes under real constraints. That is a description of judgement,
which is a postgraduate learning outcome. Forensics and network security match the Essex profile
directly; cloud, application security and enterprise-risk governance are where local options and the
capstone add flavour.

## 10. The panel already teaching the stream below (10:30 to 11:45)
The people who would teach this programme already teach the stream beneath it: an Associate Professor
from Peradeniya leading Cybersecurity II and Research Project Delivery, a senior academic from
Sri Jayewardenepura leading Networked Systems and Advanced Network Systems, four industry and two
further academic volunteers so far, and NIBM faculty for coordination and quality. Each name is proposed subject to availability and to your approval, and CVs
are ready. Structure: 180 credits, PgDip exit at 120, evening and weekend, an employer-reviewed
capstone. Entry: direct for network and cyber graduates; for other computing graduates, subject to your
approval, either a recognised professional qualification with experience, or a priced NIBM bridge, an
approach your own online programme already takes. Labs: offensive work on BYOD as at undergraduate
level, the rest on cloud-hosted ranges backed by AWS Academy and EC-Council; a dedicated facility is
requested and pending.

## 11. Risks we would rather name first (11:45 to 13:00)
Your framework asks for all risks identified, so here they are. Price is untested and 40% would need a
loan; a one-day fee-band question closes that before any forecast. Staff approval is yours; the CV pack
is ready. The physical lab is requested; launch is cloud-first. Essex Online overlap: different segment,
as the corridor showed. NCSC scope: yours to confirm, and we claim nothing until you do. The bridge is
costed, since ISC2 ended its free route in May. Two of these need your answer rather than ours.

## 12. Scale, and what we ask (13:00 to 14:15)
Your framework warns that small partnerships are neither strategic nor sustainable. So: the direct lane
alone is thin today; both lanes together clear a viable cohort; above that sit 346 recent alumni,
working professionals, the doubling direct lane and four regional campuses, none counted. No local
route today combines a UK award, certification standing, supervised labs and an evening-and-weekend
timetable. We ask three things: agreement in principle to scope the franchise; an exchange of
due-diligence packs, ours is this repository; and a joint scoping visit to Colombo within the quarter.

## 14. Questions (14:15 onward)
Likely first three: Essex Online, Coventry, NCSC. Answers below and in the appendix deck, A1 to A3.

# Question bank

**Why Essex rather than extending Coventry?** Coventry is and remains our undergraduate partner. NIBM
matches each award to the partner strongest in it. On league tables, research power and programme
profile, Essex is the fit for postgraduate cyber. We are adding a partner at a level no current
arrangement covers, not replacing one. (If pressed on Coventry's full NCSC certification: certification
is of the UK-delivered programme and does not automatically travel to a franchise for any university,
so it was not the deciding variable.)

**Does this cannibalise Essex Online?** No. The online fee is about 5.9 million rupees, roughly six
years of entry-level pay; the local field is priced ten times lower. The franchise serves buyers who
cannot reach the online fee and will not buy purely online study. It adds a segment and raises the Essex
name among the employers who later sponsor staff onto the online product.

**Can you market NCSC certification?** Not unless Essex confirms it. The campus programme is
provisionally certified to September 2027; extension to a franchise is your decision. We will claim
nothing until told what may be said.

**Why is the cyber stream so small at graduation?** Early intakes were small; year-3 entry has since
gone from 17 to 51 per intake and the degree is being reviewed and renamed. The case does not rest on
that stream alone; it rests on the whole computing base through two entry lanes.

**What will it cost a student?** Not proposed here. Price is untested and depends on your commercial
terms; a fee-band question to the same population is one day's work and is the next step.

**How do you know 15 to 35 will actually enrol?** We do not, and the slide says so. What we know is
direction: zero downward moves on the UK award across 57 people, seven independent senior voices, and a
state-sector benchmark of 28 in a comparable programme.

**Who approves teaching staff?** Essex, by name. The panel slide is proposed, not assumed; CVs are
ready, and the model is the one already delivering our Coventry stream under external oversight.

**One survey respondent said a UK master's makes no difference.** One of seven, from within the
state-university system, which is not the market this programme serves. Six of seven said the opposite.

**What is NIBM's standing to host a foreign master's award?** Handled by our partnerships team and
covered in principle by the existing Coventry arrangement; the documentation is in the verification log.
