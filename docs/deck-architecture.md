# Deck architecture and reasoning

## The organising principle
Essex publishes its own test for new partners (essex.ac.uk, "Our approach to new partnerships"):
partner reputation and academic standing; the sustained reputation of Essex as paramount, with all
risks identified; full costing for start-up and the long term; and scale, because small partnerships are
neither strategic nor sustainable. The deck is built as an answer to that test, in three acts marked in
the footer, rather than as a pitch.

## Act I: Who NIBM is (slides 2 to 4)
Statutory institution (founded 1968 with UNDP and ILO, incorporated by Act of Parliament No. 23 of
1976, state-owned under a ministry, five regional campuses); thirty years of delivering external awards
(UCD 1996, Coventry 2017, vendor academies, BCS); and the fit argument for Essex, made
concrete as a league-table and NCSC comparison against the UK awards already sold in Colombo.

The Coventry question is not raised on a slide. The portfolio slide frames NIBM as multi-partner by
design, one partner per specialism. Appendix A2 and the question bank carry the answer if asked.

## Act II: What the market says (slides 5 to 9)
The computing base and the missing rung (about 120 graduates a year, 346 in six convocations, none with
a postgraduate route at NIBM; the cyber stream small today and growing fast at year-3 entry). Demand
from the student survey (84% likely with a UK award, 23 up and none down, n = 57). The affordability
corridor (Essex campus and online fees at an order of magnitude above the local band; rupee at about
450 to the pound; entry-level pay; graduate surplus; AI compression of generalist roles). Committed
demand discounted in the open, then applied by entry lane. Industry's description of the gap in
postgraduate terms.

## Act III: How we deliver (slides 10 to 12)
The panel already teaching the undergraduate stream, proposed for the postgraduate programme subject
to availability and Essex's approval of each name; structure (MSc 180 credits, PgDip exit at 120,
evening and weekend delivery, employer-reviewed capstone); two entry lanes; laboratories (BYOD
offensive labs, cloud-hosted ranges, AWS Academy and EC-Council partnership, physical facility
requested). Risks named first, with mitigations and owners. Scale scenarios and the ask.

## Decisions taken and why
- PgDip as an exit award, not a standalone product: one cohort, one timetable; 68% of respondents want
  the MSc directly, 29% want the PgDip decision point, 4% the PgDip alone; Essex's own online portfolio
  already carries the 120-credit PgDip exit.
- No fee is proposed. Price is untested and Essex's commercial terms are unknown; the deck says so.
- The first-intake planning figure agreed in negotiation is not stated on any slide. The case shows an
  arithmetic floor (26 to 35 per intake) that sits comfortably above it.
- The one "no difference" answer in Survey B is reported in the counts and otherwise left alone; it comes
  from within the state-university system, which is not the market the programme serves.
- League-table figures use the 2026 editions consistently; refresh to 2027 before the meeting.
