# Survey A: Students and Graduates, Results

**Instrument:** MSc Cybersecurity Feasibility, Students and graduates (Microsoft Forms, 6 items, anonymous)
**Fielded:** 22 September 2026 (one response 23 September)
**Responses:** 52
**Sampling:** convenience, self-selected, single-day open link circulated within NIBM. Population denominator not fixed, so no response rate can be quoted.

---

## 1. Who responded

| Status | n | % |
|---|---|---|
| Final-year undergraduate | 22 | 42% |
| Other year undergraduate | 22 | 42% |
| Graduated (IT/cyber or other work) | 7 | 13% |
| Graduated (other work) | 1 | 2% |

Forty-four of 52 respondents (85%) are current undergraduates. This is the single most important fact about the sample, and it conditions every reading below. The survey measures a **pipeline**, not a queue of ready applicants.

Note also that the third status option combined two categories into one label, so the 7 graduates cannot be reliably split between those working in cyber and those working elsewhere. Treat all 8 graduates as one segment.

## 2. Enrolment intent

| Response | NIBM programme | With a UK award |
|---|---|---|
| Very likely | 18 | 32 |
| Somewhat likely | 19 | 11 |
| Neither | 10 | 7 |
| Somewhat unlikely | 3 | 1 |
| Very unlikely | 2 | 1 |
| **Top two combined** | **37 (71%)** | **43 (83%)** |

Mean intent rises from 3.92 to 4.38 on the five-point scale.

## 3. The UK award effect

Twenty respondents (38%) raised their stated likelihood when the award was described as coming from a UK university. **Thirty-two held steady, and not one respondent moved down.** The top box nearly doubles, from 18 to 32.

The uplift is concentrated among the hesitant rather than the already-committed. Of the 20 who moved, 14 shifted out of "somewhat likely" or lower into "very likely". Six moved from negative or neutral positions into positive ones. In other words, the UK award is what converts indifference into intent, which is exactly the argument a partnership case needs.

One caution: the UK question follows the NIBM question in the same form, so some of the gap may be an ordering effect rather than a pure preference. The direction is robust (zero downward moves), the magnitude is probably slightly overstated.

## 4. Award structure

| Preference | n | % |
|---|---|---|
| MSc directly | 36 | 69% |
| PG Dip first, then decide | 14 | 27% |
| PG Dip only | 2 | 4% |

The MSc is the demand product. The PG Diploma is not a standalone market at this sample size, but 27% want it as a decision point before committing to a full Masters. That supports a nested structure with a PG Dip exit or staging point inside the MSc, rather than two separately marketed awards.

## 5. Timing

| Earliest realistic start | n | % |
|---|---|---|
| Within 6 months | 18 | 35% |
| 6 to 12 months | 25 | 48% |
| Later | 9 | 17% |

Read this cautiously. Fourteen of the 18 who selected "within 6 months" are still undergraduates and will not have graduated in that window. The defensible reading is that roughly four in five respondents are oriented to a start within 12 months of finishing their degree, with the first genuine intake pressure landing around the 6 to 12 month mark.

## 6. Funding

| Who would mainly pay | n | % |
|---|---|---|
| Self or family | 27 | 52% |
| Would need a loan or scholarship | 21 | 40% |
| Employer | 4 | 8% |

Employer sponsorship is negligible in this sample, which follows from it being 85% students. Forty percent needing a loan or scholarship is the clearest commercial risk in the dataset, and it is unpriced, because the deployed form did not carry a fee-band question.

## 7. Committed demand

Filtering to respondents who are positive on a UK award **and** would start within 12 months gives **36 of 52 (69%)**.

Applying standard purchase-intent discounting to that group (28 "very likely" and 8 "somewhat likely"):

| Weighting | Indicative committed respondents |
|---|---|
| Unweighted | 36 |
| Central (0.8 top box, 0.3 second box) | 25 |
| Conservative (0.6 top box, 0.2 second box) | 18 |

**Recommended headline for the business case: 25 of 52, using the central weighting, stated explicitly.** Showing the discount is more persuasive than hiding it.

Within the 36: 17 self or family funded, 17 requiring a loan or scholarship, 2 employer funded. Twenty-five of the 36 want the MSc directly.

## 8. Limitations to state on the slide

The sample is self-selected and was collected in a single day from within NIBM, so it measures interest among the already-engaged, not the general market. No fee question was asked, so none of this demand is price-tested. Most respondents are current undergraduates, so timing is aspirational. Absolute numbers should be presented as indicative direction, with the discounted figure alongside the raw one, and n = 52 shown on every chart.
