# Survey A: Students and Graduates, Results (final export)

**Instrument:** MSc Cybersecurity Feasibility, Students and graduates (Microsoft Forms, 6 items, anonymous)
**Fielded:** 22 to 23 September 2026 (51 responses on 22 Sep, 6 on 23 Sep)
**Responses:** 57
**Sampling:** convenience, self-selected, open link circulated within NIBM. Population denominator not fixed, so no response rate can be quoted.

---

## 1. Who responded

| Status | n | % |
|---|---|---|
| Final-year undergraduate | 25 | 44% |
| Other year undergraduate | 24 | 42% |
| Graduated (IT/cyber or other work) | 7 | 12% |
| Graduated (other work) | 1 | 2% |

Forty-nine of 57 respondents (86%) are current undergraduates. The survey measures a pipeline, not a queue of ready applicants. The third status option combined two categories into one label, so the 7 cannot be split between cyber and other work; treat all 8 graduates as one segment.

## 2. Enrolment intent

| Response | NIBM programme | With a UK award |
|---|---|---|
| Very likely | 20 | 37 |
| Somewhat likely | 22 | 11 |
| Neither | 10 | 7 |
| Somewhat unlikely | 3 | 1 |
| Very unlikely | 2 | 1 |
| **Top two combined** | **42 (74%)** | **48 (84%)** |

Mean intent rises from 3.96 to 4.44 on the five-point scale.

## 3. The UK award effect

Twenty-three respondents (40%) raised their stated likelihood when the award was described as coming from a UK university. Thirty-four held steady, and no respondent moved down. The top box nearly doubles, from 20 to 37.

The uplift is concentrated among the hesitant: 17 of the 23 shifted into "very likely" from a lower band, and 6 moved from neutral or negative positions into positive ones.

Caution: the UK question follows the NIBM question in the same form, so part of the gap may be an ordering effect. The direction is robust (zero downward moves); the magnitude is probably slightly overstated.

## 4. Award structure

| Preference | n | % |
|---|---|---|
| MSc directly | 39 | 68% |
| PG Dip first, then decide | 16 | 28% |
| PG Dip only | 2 | 4% |

The MSc is the demand product. A PG Dip as a staged decision point inside the MSc is supported; a standalone PG Dip is not.

## 5. Timing

| Earliest realistic start | n | % |
|---|---|---|
| Within 6 months | 19 | 33% |
| 6 to 12 months | 29 | 51% |
| Later | 9 | 16% |

Fifteen of the 19 who selected "within 6 months" are still undergraduates. The defensible reading: about five in six respondents are oriented to a start within 12 months of finishing their degree, with the first real intake pressure at the 6 to 12 month mark.

## 6. Funding

| Who would mainly pay | n | % |
|---|---|---|
| Self or family | 30 | 53% |
| Would need a loan or scholarship | 23 | 40% |
| Employer | 4 | 7% |

Forty percent needing a loan or scholarship is the clearest commercial risk in the dataset, and it is unpriced: the deployed form carried no fee-band question.

## 7. Committed demand

Positive on a UK award and able to start within 12 months: **41 of 57 (72%)**, of whom 33 are "very likely" and 8 "somewhat likely".

| Weighting | Indicative committed respondents | Rate applied in the deck |
|---|---|---|

| Conservative (0.6 top box, 0.2 second box) | 21.4, shown as 21 | 37.5% (21.4 / 57) |

Within the 41: 20 self or family funded, 19 requiring a loan or scholarship, 2 employer funded; 28 want the MSc directly, 12 the PG Dip first, 1 the PG Dip only.

## 8. Limitations to state

Self-selected, collected in two days from within NIBM, so it measures interest among the already engaged. No fee question, so none of this demand is price-tested. Mostly undergraduates, so timing is aspirational. Present absolute numbers as direction, with the discounted figure beside the raw one and n = 57 on every chart.
